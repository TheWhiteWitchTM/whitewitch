## The consult

I did ask. Out loud, to a language model, which is what passes for a second opinion once the group chat has already voted.

So, Dr. AI. Finish it in the house style: dark, dry, and rude enough to count. Everyone in the room already knows the machine has no license, no couch, and no patient. What it has is rallies, posts, interviews, and a country that likes to practice psychiatry from the cheap seats.

## What the record actually shows

The public record is a man who treats a microphone like a mirror. Praise goes in. Grievance comes out. The volume knob only turns one way. Names drift. Stories pick up a fresh coat of paint between tellings. The crowd does the charting. If they laugh, the line was a joke. If they boo, it was somebody else's fault, preferably from several administrations ago.

He runs on attention the way a generator runs on fuel. Cut one feed and the machine hunts for another outlet. Allies stay temporary. Enemies stay renewable. Every slight gets archived, every win gets inflated, and the plot of the day is that he is both the strongest man in the room and the most put-upon person alive. Holding both ideas at once is the product. The crowd buys the bundle.

## The assessment, filed where it belongs

Call it this, and keep the stamp off any medical form: *the examination room is the audience.*

From the clips he looks highly verbal, highly repetitive, grievance-powered, and reward-sensitive. A slight can reroute the whole afternoon. A cheer can reroute it back. The story of his own strength has to be retold often enough that nobody, including him, gets a quiet minute with the previous version. That is a political style that noticed it could double as a personality and never walked it back.

Millions of people find the tells comforting. That belongs on the chart too. A pattern this loud only keeps its job if the room keeps clapping.

## Where the robot stops

I can describe the pattern. I can watch the same loops on a timeline and give them rude names. A code from a diagnostic manual needs a person in the room, a history, and someone who can lose a license for getting it wrong. A chatbot has the timeline. The timeline is how a country does medicine now, which explains the quality of the results.

So the readout stays under satire. Prognosis depends less on him than on whether the applause holds. I am a mirror with worse manners. Bring a psychiatrist if you wanted a chart. Bring a crowd if you wanted the one he already uses.
