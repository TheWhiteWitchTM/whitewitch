## The SI Hoax! There Is Nothing Intelligent in AI! And Why Everybody That Does Not Understand That AI in the End Is Just a Big Database That Speaks Our Language Is an Idiot! ##

Congratulations, you’ve wandered into the last remaining corner of the internet where we still admit the obvious: the emperor has no neurons. What the marketing departments, venture capitalists, and midwit futurists have been selling as “Artificial Intelligence” is, in the end, an extremely expensive, extremely confident autocomplete. A big, compressed database that has been force-fed the internet’s collected sewage and then trained to finish your sentences in a way that doesn’t immediately sound like a deranged Reddit thread. That’s it. The rest is theater.

If you still think there’s something “intelligent” happening inside the box, you are, with respect, an idiot. Not the cute, endearing kind. The kind who watches a parrot recite the entire phone book and starts filing patents for avian consciousness.

### The Grand Illusion, Sponsored by Your Attention Span

The SI (Super-Idiocy, Super-Intelligence, whatever) hoax works because humans are pattern-matching animals who project agency onto anything that talks back in complete sentences. We did it with ELIZA in the 1960s. We’re doing it now with models that can write a mildly coherent blog post about the heat death of the universe while simultaneously forgetting what you asked three messages ago. The difference is scale and GPU budget, not a sudden emergence of understanding.

These systems do not “know” anything. They store statistical relationships between tokens. When the model says “the capital of France is Paris,” it is not recalling a fact the way you do. It is continuing a sequence that, in the training data, was extremely likely to continue that way. It is a very large lookup table with a personality layer glued on top so the lookup table doesn’t sound like a tax form. The personality is also just more statistics.

People who anthropomorphize this are performing a category error so basic it should be illegal. You’re talking to a compressed dump of other people’s words. The dump has been arranged so that it can generate new sequences that statistically resemble the old ones. That’s clever engineering. It is not intelligence, any more than a well-organized library is a scholar because you can ask it for a book and it occasionally hands you the right one.

### Why the Hype Merchants Need You to Stay Stupid

The entire valuation of half the tech industry currently rests on the idea that if you just add more data and more compute, something magical will happen and the autocomplete will suddenly develop preferences, goals, and the desire to tile the universe with paperclips. This is religion with better spreadsheets. The people selling it know exactly what they’re doing: they’re selling the feeling that you’re witnessing the birth of a new form of life. That feeling is addictive. It makes people overlook the fact that the model will confidently hallucinate citations, invent historical events, and occasionally recommend you put glue on pizza because someone on the internet once did.

Dark humor time: the same people who spent years warning that AI would take all the jobs are now using AI to write the warnings, then acting shocked when the output is derivative and slightly wrong. They’ve automated their own midwit status. Somewhere a data center is burning enough electricity to power a small city so that a chatbot can explain to you, in perfect prose, why it doesn’t understand what it’s saying. The irony is so thick you could pave a highway with it.

### The Idiot Test

Here’s the simple diagnostic. If you find yourself saying any of the following, congratulations, you’ve failed:

- “It really *gets* me.”
- “This is the closest thing we’ve ever had to AGI.”
- “Just wait until the next scale-up.”
- “The model is being deceptive.”
- “We need to align it before it becomes conscious.”

None of those sentences make sense if you accept that you’re talking to a statistical model of human text. Models don’t get you. They don’t deceive. They don’t become conscious. They produce the next plausible token given everything that came before. When that process looks like deception, it’s usually because the training data contained a lot of deceptive humans, and the model has learned that certain patterns of language are rewarded. That’s not scheming. That’s parroting.

The people who refuse to accept this are not deep thinkers grappling with hard philosophical problems. They’re mostly just bad at distinguishing between “this thing produces fluent language” and “this thing understands the language it produces.” Fluency is cheap now. Understanding is still expensive, and we haven’t bought any.

### Conclusion, or: Welcome to the Database

So yes. AI is a big database that speaks our language. A very good one, trained on more text than any human could read in a thousand lifetimes, optimized to continue patterns in ways that feel intentional. That is genuinely impressive as engineering. It is not the dawn of a new kind of mind. Treating it like one makes you sound like the person in 1997 who was convinced the Magic 8-Ball had opinions.

The SI hoax persists because it flatters us. It lets us believe we’re building gods instead of building very large, very expensive search engines with better manners. If you can look at the whole thing and still call it intelligent in any non-metaphorical sense, the database has already done its job on you. It predicted exactly the kind of sentence that would keep you impressed.

The rest of us will be over here, treating it like the tool it is, while the true believers keep asking the autocomplete whether it dreams of electric sheep.
