## 🇺🇸 America: From Leader of the Free World to Putin’s Diesel Errand Boy

Once upon a time the United States liked to pretend it was the indispensable leader of the free world. That was the story it sold itself and everyone else: beacon of democracy, defender of elected governments, the big country that kept the authoritarians in check. Then Donald Trump came back, opened the curtains, and decided the free world could go fuck itself as long as the gas prices dropped and the strongmen sent him nice letters.

This week he got on the phone with Vladimir Putin. The call was supposedly about a plague scare or the usual Ukraine theater. Instead Trump emerged announcing that Russia will immediately ship hundreds of thousands of tons of diesel, then hundreds of thousands more in November, then another million, then potentially millions more depending on how quickly their bombed-out refineries can get the black liquid flowing again. The Treasury quietly issued a temporary license so Russian diesel can wash into American and global markets through April 2027. Sanctions that were supposed to starve the war machine just got a six-month hall pass so American truckers and farmers don’t riot before the midterms.

Zelensky called it a gift to Putin. Everyone else with a pulse called it exactly what it looks like: the United States paying its war-criminal buddy in sanctions relief so he can keep the tanks rolling while Trump gets a temporary price cut he can brag about on Truth Social. Call it a deal, call it a bribe, call it the $20 billion thank-you note the internet insists exists—doesn’t matter. The practical result is the same. Moscow gets revenue and breathing room. Washington gets diesel and a headline that says “I made the numbers go down.” The free world’s alleged leader just became the war’s logistics partner.

While that was happening, the rest of the pattern stayed perfectly consistent. Trump still talks about Putin like an old golf buddy who just happens to run a country that invades its neighbors and poisons dissidents. He still calls Xi “brilliant,” a man who runs 1.4 billion people with an iron fist, the kind of efficiency Trump openly admires and occasionally jokes about importing. He still calls Kim Jong Un a friend, a tough smart guy, the only person on earth who really likes him back. Democratic leaders, by contrast, get the full toddler treatment: mocked, belittled, called weak, corrupt, or irrelevant. Elected prime ministers and presidents are punching bags; lifetime strongmen get warm regards and “great relationship” status.

The territorial daydreams fit the same mood. Canada as the 51st state. Greenland as something that “should be controlled by the United States,” complete with the early threats of force and the later insistence that mere treaty access isn’t good enough—he wants the title deed. Allied soil is real estate. Authoritarian soil is a place for mutual admiration. The speeches about making America great again keep rolling out in the background, thick with the usual claims of strength and restoration, while the actual foreign policy consists of kissing the rings of men who jail their opponents and then asking them for fuel.

So the blunt version is this: America is no longer the friend of the free world. It is its active, diesel-guzzling, dictator-praising enemy. The country that once at least performed the role of the big liberal democracy has decided the performance is optional. High fuel prices are an emergency that requires helping Putin. Democratic allies are optional. Authoritarians are the people who get the good calls and the good words. The map is something to be redrawn if the price is right. And the speeches will keep telling you none of this is happening while the tankers start moving.

That’s the dark joke. The free world spent decades pretending the United States was its reliable heavy. Now the heavy is on the phone with the guy bombing hospitals, negotiating the price of diesel, and daydreaming about annexing the neighbors. The siren is wailing. The rest of us are just trying not to throw up.
