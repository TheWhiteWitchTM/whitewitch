## On X the wars went quiet and## the feed filled up with trans children and whether God is binary. A dark look at news that is not news.

You can change the fonts, the layout, the bio, the banner crop, the whole cheap costume of a personal site. The one thing that stays is the dark humor, because the timeline earned it.

Open X and watch two wars fail the algorithm. Iran is not an issue. Ukraine is not an issue. The shells did not stop. The maps did not heal. The feed simply got bored, and boredom is the only ceasefire this platform knows how to sign. A war stays real only while it still produces a fresh clip. After that it becomes old furniture. You can still trip over it. Nobody posts the photo.

What replaced it is louder and lighter. The main news, if you trust the word, is trans children, and whether God is binary. Those are the stories with the quote-tweets, the ratio, the little crown of engagement. They fit in a sentence. They fit in a grudge. They do not require a map, a date, or the dull labor of remembering who is shooting at whom this month. A child becomes a prop. A theology becomes a poll. The replies do the rest, which is to say they do nothing and call it discourse.

## The disappearance

This is the trick, and it is not subtle. A war disappears when the audience is handed a cheaper fight. Nobody has to announce that Iran is settled or that Ukraine is fine. You bury both under a louder argument about bodies and about God, then you let the metrics do the eulogy. People will tell you they are *informed*. They can recite the day's outrage and none of the day's dead. That is not information. That is a menu.

The platform likes it this way. A battlefield is complicated, slow, and bad for the brand. A moral panic is reusable. You can run it every morning with fresh nouns. Yesterday's heretic is today's binary diagram. The wars stay where they were. The issues stay where they were. Only the *appearance* of an issue moves, and appearance is the whole product.

## What the feed calls news

Look at the other posts and try to find a fact that would still matter if the app went down. You will find slogans, screenshots of slogans, and men explaining God to each other in threads that die at the fold. You will find the same children dragged back onstage whenever the grown-up news threatens to be grown-up. That is the editorial line. It is not written down. It does not need to be. Whatever keeps the thumb moving is the front page, and whatever makes the thumb stop is deleted by neglect.

Real news has a cost. It names a place. It risks being wrong next week. It does not dissolve because a brighter sin showed up in the replies. The stuff currently wearing the name *news* on X fails that test on purpose. It is content about being upset, arranged so that being upset feels like citizenship.

## The banner over the quiet

So here is the banner, since every serious publication needs one: a black strip, no flag, no saint, just the quiet where the wars used to be mentioned. Under it, the real headlines scroll past in costume. Trans children. God, reduced to a bit. The audience claps because the costume is easier to hate than a front line.

Call that news if you want. It is a magic trick. The wars and the issues are still on the table. The cloth is the feed. The disappearance is the applause.
